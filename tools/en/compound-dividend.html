A projection, not a promise. Returns and dividends are not guaranteed, and taxes, fees and inflation are not included.

How the calculation works

The annual price return is converted to a monthly rate with (1 + r)^(1/12) − 1 rather than r ÷ 12, so twelve months compound to exactly the annual figure. Dividends are calculated on the current balance each month and either added back to it (DRIP) or accumulated as cash. Contributions are added at the end of each month.

Price growth and dividends are entered separately because they are different things: a fund can rise 6% a year and distribute another 3%. With DRIP on, the two compound together; with it off, you see the income you would actually receive.

What to put in each field

  • Initial investment — what you already hold, in the currency you select.
  • Monthly contribution — the amount you add every month, starting with the first one.
  • Price return — average annual price growth you expect. 5–8% is a common long-run assumption for global equities; history is not a guarantee.
  • Dividend yield — the annual distribution as a percentage of the balance. Broad index funds are often 1–2%, higher-dividend strategies 3–5%.
  • DRIP — on, dividends buy more of the same investment; off, they are paid out as cash.

Reading the table

Each row is one year. “Invested” is cumulative money you put in — your cost basis. “Dividends to date” is everything the investment has paid you. “End balance” is what the portfolio is worth when dividends were reinvested; with DRIP off, the cash column shows what has been paid out instead, and total wealth is the balance plus that cash.

Frequently asked questions

What does DRIP mean?

Dividend reinvestment plan. Instead of taking the dividend as cash, it buys more of the same investment, so the next dividend is paid on a larger balance. Over decades this is the difference between linear and exponential growth.

Should DRIP be on or off?

Turn it off to see the income you could live on; turn it on to see long-term growth. Most total-return projections assume reinvestment.

Does it account for taxes?

No. Depending on your country, dividend and capital gains taxes can take 15–30% or more of the gain, and fund fees take a little every year. Subtract them if you want a realistic figure.

How is the monthly rate calculated?

With the twelfth root of the annual rate: (1 + r)^(1/12) − 1. Simply dividing by twelve slightly overstates the yearly result.

What return is realistic?

Nobody knows in advance. Run the calculation two or three times — at 4%, 7% and 10%, say — and plan around the pessimistic case rather than the best one.

Which currency should I use?

Whichever you think in. The calculator only formats the numbers; it does not convert between currencies.

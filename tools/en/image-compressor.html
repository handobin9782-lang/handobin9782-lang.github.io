How to use it

  1. Add your imagesDrag files onto the box or tap it to browse. Several files can be handled at once, and each gets its own download button.
  2. Choose format and qualityJPG is the safest choice for photographs, WebP usually gives the smallest file, and PNG is for screenshots, logos and anything with transparency. Move the quality slider down until the preview still looks right.
  3. Compare and downloadEach result shows the original size, the new size and the saved percentage. Download files one by one, or use “Download all”.

Which format should I choose?

  • JPG — best for photos and anything that will be shared. It discards detail the eye barely notices, which is why a 4 MB photo often becomes 300 KB.
  • WebP — typically 25–35% smaller than JPG at the same visual quality, and it supports transparency. Every current browser can display it.
  • PNG — lossless and transparent, but large for photographs. Use it for screenshots, logos, icons and images with sharp text.

What the quality slider does

Quality controls the encoder, not the pixel dimensions. At 80% most photos look identical to the original while being several times smaller; below 50% you start to see blocky areas in skies and smooth gradients. PNG ignores the slider because it is lossless — if you need a smaller PNG, convert it to WebP.

Privacy details

Re-encoding through the canvas removes EXIF metadata, including the GPS coordinates, camera model and capture time that phones embed in photos. That is usually what you want before posting a picture publicly, but keep the original file if you need the metadata.

Frequently asked questions

Are my images uploaded anywhere?

No. Images are decoded and re-encoded by your own browser. Nothing is sent over the network, and the tool keeps working offline once the page has loaded.

Why did my PNG get bigger?

PNG is lossless and the quality slider does not apply to it, so re-encoding can add a few bytes. Convert photos to WebP or JPG for a real reduction.

Is EXIF or GPS data removed?

Yes — the canvas re-encode drops EXIF, GPS and other metadata. Keep a copy of the original if you need that information.

What is the maximum file size?

There is no fixed limit; the practical one is your device's memory. A 20–30 MB photo is fine on a recent phone, while very large images may take a few seconds.

Does it work with HEIC photos from an iPhone?

iPhones usually save as HEIC, which browsers cannot always decode. Set the camera to “Most Compatible” or export as JPG first, and the file will work here.

Why these tools run in your browser

Most “free online converters” upload your file to a server, convert it there and send it back. Your photos and contracts then sit on someone else's disk, and you are trusting a stranger's retention policy. These tools take a different route: the code is downloaded to you, and the work happens on your own device.

For images that is the Canvas API, for PDFs it is the open-source pdf-lib library, and for text it is plain JavaScript. Because nothing is transmitted, the pages keep working when your connection drops — and they are fast, since there is no upload and no queue.

Frequently asked questions

Do I have to pay or create an account?

No. Every tool is free and works without an account, an email address or a login.

Are my files uploaded to a server?

No. Files are read and processed inside the browser tab. You can disconnect from the internet after the page loads and the tools still work.

Do the tools work on a phone?

Yes. The layout adapts to small screens and the image compressor handles photos taken on a phone; the only practical limit is the device's memory.

Why is the site in several languages?

The same tools are published in English, Japanese, Spanish, German and Korean, with a language switcher at the top of every page.

Is the compound calculator financial advice?

No. It is a projection based on the return and dividend yield you enter. Real markets vary, and taxes, fees and inflation are not included.

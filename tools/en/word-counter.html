What counts as a word?

For space-separated languages, a word is any run of letters, digits or apostrophes — the same rule word processors use, so quotes and numbers are not counted twice. Japanese text has no spaces, so the page uses the browser's built-in word segmenter (Intl.Segmenter) and the count is a good approximation rather than an exact dictionary parse.

Why bytes matter

Many systems limit bytes, not characters. SMS allows 160 bytes in a single GSM message; a database column or an API field may be capped at 2,000 bytes. In UTF-8 a plain English letter is one byte, most accented letters are two, and Japanese, Korean and Chinese characters and emoji are three or four. A 500-character Japanese text is therefore about 1,500 bytes — worth checking before pasting into a form that counts bytes.

Reading and speaking time

Reading time assumes 200 words per minute for space-separated languages and 500 characters per minute for Japanese, the usual range for silent reading of average text. Speaking time uses 130 words per minute, a comfortable presentation pace. Both are estimates: dense technical text is slower, simple dialogue faster.

Frequently asked questions

Is my text stored or sent anywhere?

No. Counting happens in the page with JavaScript. Nothing is transmitted, saved or logged, and the tool keeps working offline.

How are Japanese and Chinese characters counted?

Characters are counted exactly; the word count uses the browser's segmenter and is an approximation. For translation pricing, characters or bytes are the more reliable unit.

Why do bytes and characters differ?

A character is a code point; a byte is how much storage it takes in UTF-8. English letters take one byte, Japanese, Korean and Chinese characters three, and emoji four.

Can I use it for SEO descriptions?

Yes. The table above shows the common limits; Google typically truncates a meta description around 155–160 characters.

Does it count spaces and line breaks?

Characters with spaces includes them; the “no spaces” figure and the word count ignore them. Line breaks count as characters in both figures.
